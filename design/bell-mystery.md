# The Missing Festival Bell — design (agreed 2026-11-21, not built yet)

An optional Clue-style mystery, played in **English** (not a Ukrainian lesson). Text only, no new recordings.

## The question
The festival bell has been stolen. Vicky works out three things:
1. **Who** took it: one of the six visitors (Myron, Yaroslava, Ostap, Lesia, Zakhar, Halyna).
2. **Where** it is hidden: the windmill, the train station, the church, the lake's reeds, the wheat field, or under the bridge.
3. **With what** it was carried off: wheelbarrow, horse and cart, mule, pull sled, ox (a sixth still to choose).

The answer is **random each time** a new mystery starts; every clue is generated to fit it.

## How it starts
The visitors gossip about it the morning it happens ("Did you hear? The festival bell is gone!"). Talking to any of them opens the case.

## Talking to the visitors
- **Dialogue trees:** each talk offers 2–3 things for Vicky to say ("Where were you this morning?", "Did you see anything odd?", "What do you think of Ostap?"). Their answers give alibis and sightings. New questions unlock as she learns things, so a clue from one person gives her something to ask another.
- **Suggest, Clue-style:** in any talk Vicky can say "I think it was Ostap, at the mill, with the wheelbarrow." That visitor then reveals **one** thing they know rules it out ("It wasn't the mill, I was there all morning"), or says they can't disprove it.
- Each visitor secretly "holds" a few of the innocent cards (like Clue's dealt hand), which decides what they can disprove and what their alibis and sightings mention.

## Detective notebook
A Clue sheet: rows for the six suspects, six places and six methods. Clues are noted automatically; Vicky can tick things off as ruled out or mark them likely.

## Accusing
A **police officer** stands just above the fountain. Vicky tells him who, where and with what.
- **Right:** "Ah, I have a hunch you're correct!" The bell is found, and the culprit explains (lightly, never anything dark).
- **Wrong:** "I'll look into it, but I have my doubts..." She can accuse again **tomorrow** (one accusation a day).

## Settled since
- Sixth method: the **hay wagon**.
- The officer: **Officer Petro**, a kindly, round, moustached, slow-talking village constable in a blue uniform and cap, with a notebook. Doubtful but fair.
- Reward: a **golden bell trophy** for Vicky's cottage.

## The writing (the user's rule)
Nothing on the nose. Clues are hints Vicky has to read and connect, never answers. Nobody says "it was the wheelbarrow"; someone mentions a single narrow wheel track in the mud by the mill, or deep, split hoof prints (an ox), or hay caught on a fence. People notice small, specific, physical things, and each speaks in their own voice (Myron's booming certainty, Yaroslava's poetic asides, Ostap's excited exaggeration, Lesia's eye for colour, Zakhar's few dry words, Halyna's gossip that's half right). Some things are only clear once two clues are put together. The writing should be good: a little mystery, a little humour, never dark.
